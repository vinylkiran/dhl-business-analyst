This portfolio covers five end-to-end BA/DA projects delivered across DHL's Illinois, New Jersey, and Texas warehouse network. Each project follows the same methodology: define the business problem precisely, explore the data with SQL, derive KPIs with documented formulas, and deliver an interactive dashboard alongside written stakeholder documents.

Collectively, the five projects create a connected analytics foundation covering inventory management, demand planning, customer retention, warehouse operations, and performance monitoring — moving from fragmented, site-specific Excel tracking to a standardised, repeatable analytical framework.

03
Project 01 · Inventory Management
SKU Segmentation & Inventory Optimisation
Problem
2,000 SKUs across three warehouses managed with a single, undifferentiated safety stock policy. Over-investment in C-class slow movers; under-buffering of high-revenue AX SKUs causing stockouts and lost revenue.
Approach
ABC classification (revenue contribution) × XYZ classification (demand variability) to create a 2×3 segmentation matrix. Derived differentiated safety stock multipliers and reorder points per segment using the statistical formula SS = Z × σ × √(Lead Time).
Finding 1
164 A-class SKUs generate ~70% of revenue. 49 AX SKUs are the highest-priority replenishment items — predictable, high-value, zero tolerance for stockout.
Finding 2
1,048 CY SKUs represent the over-investment risk: low revenue, moderate variability, high catalogue count. Candidates for safety stock reduction or rationalisation.
Finding 3
AZ SKUs (erratic, high-value) have the highest stockout risk in the portfolio. Standard moving-average forecasting is unsuitable — requires probabilistic or simulation-based safety stock.
Business Impact
Differentiated SS rules reduce working capital tied up in C-class overstock while protecting A-class fill rates. Estimated annual value of improved stock positioning: ~$150K (reduced excess stock carrying cost + reduced A-class stockout cost).

Project 02 · Demand Planning
Demand Forecasting & Pipeline Requirements
Problem
No standardised forecast methodology across the planning team. No measurable baseline to evaluate any future improvement. No pipeline specification to enable automation.
Approach
Established a 14-day moving average as the baseline model. Calculated MAPE per ABC/XYZ segment on an out-of-sample 30-day test window. Derived category seasonality indices. Wrote a formal pipeline requirements document for the DE team.
Finding 1
AX SKUs achieve MAPE of 12–14% with a simple 14-day MA — within acceptable range for operational planning. No complex model needed for the most predictable items.
Finding 2
Consumer Electronics and Fashion & Apparel show seasonality indices of 1.3–1.4 in Q4. Safety stock should be increased by ~30–40% entering the peak season for these categories.
Finding 3
Z-class SKUs are unsuitable for moving average methods (MAPE > 50%). Probabilistic safety stock methods required. This is the highest-priority modelling task for the DS team.

Project 03 · Customer Analytics
RFM Segmentation & Retention A/B Test
Problem
398 active customers managed reactively. No framework for identifying which customers were at risk of churn before they churned, or which were highest priority for growth investment.
Approach
RFM scoring (Recency × Frequency × Monetary) across 24 months of order history. Seven-segment taxonomy. A/B test designed for the "At Risk" segment (46 customers) targeting 90-day re-order conversion.
Finding 1
Potential Loyalists (151 customers) are the highest-value growth target — already engaged, not yet fully committed. The right nudge (personalised offer, dedicated account contact) could convert them to Loyal tier.
Finding 2
Champions and Loyal Customers (70 total) have OTIF rates 3–5pp above the base average — confirming that operational service quality is a retention driver for top customers.
Finding 3
A/B test result: NOT statistically significant (p=0.555). Recommend extending campaign duration to 120 days and increasing control group size before drawing conclusions.

Project 04 · Warehouse Efficiency
Warehouse Slotting Optimisation
Problem
High-velocity Hot SKUs stored in suboptimal Reserve/Bulk zones; low-velocity Cold SKUs occupying premium Pick_Face space. Operators travel further per pick on the highest-frequency items, inflating labour cost.
Approach
Frequency-based slotting: Hot/Warm/Cold tiers derived from 24-month pick frequency percentiles. Labour saving calculated from zone-level Duration_Min differentials. SKU affinity analysis for co-location recommendations. Three-phase implementation plan.
Finding 1
290 SKUs misplaced (17.4% mismatch rate): 167 Hot SKUs not in Pick_Face, 123 Cold SKUs blocking Pick_Face capacity. Highest-value misplacement: Consumer Electronics and FMCG A-class SKUs in Bulk storage.
Finding 2
Total programme ROI: 5.8×. Break-even: 125 days. Implementation cost: $3,838. Labour saving over 24 months: $22,414. Slotting optimisation alone (Phase 1) delivers ROI of 9.1×.
Finding 3
WRONG_LOCATION errors are highest at TX03 (103 incidents) — consistent with recent slotting changes. This is a transition artefact expected to resolve within 60 days of implementation completion.

Project 05 · Operational Monitoring
WMS Operational Dashboard
Problem
Three site managers tracking KPIs in incompatible local Excel files. Network-level performance invisible to VP Operations. No early warning before SLA breaches. Data quality issues (high-error SKUs, operator spikes) never surfaced.
Approach
Standardised KPI definitions across all three sites in a formal KPI dictionary. DuckDB analysis of 219,000 WMS tasks. Five-section Chart.js dashboard. Operator scorecard (180 operators), data quality monitoring layer (34 flags).
Finding 1
Network pick accuracy 99.27%, putaway 99.70% — both GREEN. Cycle count accuracy 98.49% is AMBER at two sites, the portfolio's primary operational concern and leading indicator of future IRA risk.
Finding 2
20 SKUs flagged with persistent error rates above 2% for 3+ consecutive months — product-level issues (labelling, packaging) rather than operator error. Quality team review required.
Finding 3
All 180 operators above the 98.5% coaching threshold. Four high performers (≥99.8%) identified — three showing declining trend in H2, requiring proactive retention of high-performer status.
